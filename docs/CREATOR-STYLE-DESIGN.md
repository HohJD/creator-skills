# Creator-first voice design

This is a **public, generalized** description of why the script skill defaults sound the way they do. It is not an unpublished script collection or an attempt to impersonate an identifiable individual.

## Observed editorial pattern translated into constraints

- Open on a real, attention-worthy result, not inflated product marketing.
- Let the narrator sound as though they tried something personally, but only when that experience is documented.
- Follow short steps viewers can actually see: choose/open/upload/generate/check.
- Make the result concrete. If it is AI-generated cinematic imagery, say so.
- Close with one specific resource-led CTA such as the exact prompt/template/workflow, not three generic engagement asks.
- Preserve natural spoken pacing and everyday vocabulary; use enthusiasm when supported by the result.
- Reject self-centred or cringeworthy framing if it detracts from the viewer's curiosity.
- In sponsored work, begin with a genuine demonstration, then identify how the product enabled it, keeping disclosure and mandatory copy accurate.
- Adapt to XHS conversational Mandarin as a new voiceover, not a literal English subtitle translation.

## Distinction from universal advice

These are editorial patterns, not experimentally established universal conditions for engagement. The evidence-backed basis for visuals, worked examples, segmentation, cognitive load and narration is explained in [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md). Specific pacing, hook and CTA choices require A/B tests and viewer task-success data.

## Personal style data is not public

A user's *private* voice profile goes in `.tutorial-local/voice-profile.md`. The repository `.gitignore` excludes that directory; it must never be bundled into public distribution without explicit approval. Follow the author-provenance guidance in the `voice-profile` mode of `scriptcraft` to avoid treating assistant drafts as creator-owned voice evidence.
