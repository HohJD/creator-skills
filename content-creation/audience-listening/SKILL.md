# Audience listening

Start with the decision the creator wants to make. Inventory the observations, collection window, channel and unit of counting. Keep a stable source ID for each item. Label a comment, a person and a thread separately when the distinction affects a count.

Group observations by the job a viewer struggles with. A single item can support multiple themes; say when totals overlap. Keep unusual or contradictory experiences if they change the editorial choice. Separate what someone actually said from what you infer they need. Strip private handles and avoid public-facing quotes from private messages without permission. Do not treat a convenience sample as a survey, infer demographics or label a small spike a trend.

Return a theme, supporting IDs, a defensible count where possible, a question the next piece could answer, and one way to test demand. Prefer a tight brief whose answer is filmable or verifiable. If the user wants replies to individual viewers, use a community-reply workflow instead.

## Copyable brief

> Use [comments or queries] from [channel/window] to find three reader needs. Show which items support each, a counterexample where relevant, and one piece we can make next.

## Worked example

**Fictional input.** C1 asks which side of a patch faces out. C2 asks for a close-up of its backing. C3 asks where to buy the cloth.

**First piece:** photograph both faces of the patch and label the one that faces the garment. C1 and C2 suggest a need to recognise the material, although only C1 explicitly asks about orientation. C3 is a sourcing question. Report two related comments out of three, with no claim about how many customers are confused.
