# Short clip selection

Work from the supplied media or transcript. If you can review the recording, listen through the context on both sides of the candidate. A transcript alone can support a paper edit with anchors or exact phrases. Never claim a facial expression, clean audio, frame-accurate boundary or visual result you haven't observed.

Find a complete exchange, decision, demonstration or surprise. Check the line just before the proposed opening for missing pronouns and the line just after the proposed ending for a qualifier or reversal. Keep the question when the answer needs it. Avoid cuts that create a new meaning by moving two fragments together. If shortening would lose a crucial caveat, pick another moment or use the longer contiguous section.

Give the requested number of clips in priority order, with source file, in/out or verbatim locator, first and last words, the point of the clip and any necessary context card. Distinguish confirmed boundaries from coarse anchors. Count duration only when both ends are known. If asked for the edited media, inspect the rendered cut when the source and tools are available.

## Try it

> Choose [number] clips from [transcript or recording] for [audience]. Give a paper edit with source locators, the opening and closing words, and anything I'd misrepresent by cutting too early.

## Worked example

Fictional source: in paragraph 4, a maker says, “The glue held overnight. By lunchtime the join had slipped.” The brief wants a short clip answering whether the repair held.

**Select:** Both sentences of paragraph 4, from “The glue” through “had slipped.” The turn between the two observations is the story. A text card can identify the joint from the supplied project notes. With only paragraph locators, leave the final in/out for the edit.
