# Storyboards and continuity

Work from the actual script, intended viewer, framing and available assets. Identify the thing that physically changes, the moment the audience learns it, and any object or screen state that must persist. Build panels in viewing order. A shoot-day schedule belongs in the shot-list workflow.

For each panel give the starting frame, action or change, ending frame, sound or line, and the cut into the next shot. Re-establish screen direction when the viewpoint changes. For screen demos, state what the cursor or finger starts on and what becomes visible. When useful, provide a still prompt per panel with repeated character, prop and palette details. Do not represent an illustration as an actual result or add camera movement that the supplied setup cannot make.

Read the panels without dialogue. Then listen to the lines without the panels. Repair any handoff in which the viewer cannot tell what changed or the narration repeats an action that was already obvious. If durations are requested, distinguish planned timing from measured footage.

## Copyable brief

> Storyboard [scene] into [number] panels. Track the prop state and screen direction. Return start frame, action, end frame, audio and cut for each.

## Worked example

**Fictional input.** A locked overhead phone records a sealed red envelope. A hand enters from the right, tears the top and removes a blue card.

| Panel | Action and end state | Sound and handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sealed envelope centred, hand enters from the right and holds the top. | Start on room tone. Keep the opening edge visible. |
| 2 | Same overhead view. The hand tears the top. End with the opening visible. | Let the tear play. Cut only after the edge separates. |
| 3 | From that opening, slide the blue card to the right. Hold on card and torn envelope. | Use the paper slide; let the reveal land. |

The panels can all come from a single continuous take.
