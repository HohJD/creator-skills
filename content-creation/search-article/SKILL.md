# Search-led articles

Identify the question behind the query, reader starting point, desired action and what original value the creator can offer. Distinguish a how-to from a comparison, definition or troubleshooting page. Research changeable claims using current primary sources when browsing is allowed. Never fabricate a search volume, keyword difficulty, ranking, personal test or source.

When asked for a full article, write the article. Put a usable answer or decision rule near the top, then provide evidence, steps and exceptions in the order a reader needs them. Keep headings descriptive. Put references beside the claims they support. Use alternatives only when the reader faces a real choice. For an editorial brief instead, list the angle, reader questions, evidence to obtain and proposed headings.

If requested, supply a descriptive title and a distinct meta description that reflects the page. A missing verified detail can be omitted or marked for the editor outside the article. Apply length targets as editorial requirements instead of treating them as ranking factors.

## Copyable brief

> Write [article or brief] for someone searching [question]. Use [original observations/sources]. Answer the question early, show what the reader can verify, and keep unsupported product claims out.

## Worked example

**Fictional input.** A studio guide asks how to identify the right end of its paper roll. The creator's two photos show a blue tab on the loose end and no tab on the core.

**Title:** Where to start our studio paper roll

**Opening:** Find the blue tab on the outside of the roll. It marks the loose end. The centre in the second photo is the core; leave that in place.

Use a labelled crop of each supplied photo alongside the instruction. Keep the claim specific to the studio's roll.
