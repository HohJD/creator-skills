# Prepublication fact check

Pull out consequential, checkable claims from the headline, overlays, spoken copy and description. Prioritize numbers, comparisons, quotations, prices, access, dates and promises. Establish the version, account, region, date and unit that matter. Separate opinion from a falsifiable statement. Do not treat a quoted sponsor line, AI answer, search snippet or repeated press release as independent verification.

For time-sensitive or externally testable facts, inspect the original source or observation when access permits. Open the cited page far enough to check what it actually says. Record the date and relevant passage or locator, and distinguish it from a creator's local test. If sources disagree, show the conflict and narrow the copy. If the user requests an offline review, use supplied evidence and flag what still needs checking.

Return a compact claim ledger with original words, status, evidence, exact replacement and what could resolve any remaining uncertainty. Keep supported lines. Never invent a source, quote or date to fill a gap. A missing source alone warrants “unverified,” not “false.” Preserve any required wording separately if it conflicts with the evidence and needs the sponsor's decision.

## Try it

> Audit [draft plus materials]. Which claims can I publish as written? For each consequential issue, give me the evidence and the smallest accurate replacement. Mark what still needs a source.

## Worked example

Fictional brief: “Every export is finished in ten seconds.” Evidence: one timed screen recording of a 12-second clip exporting in ten seconds on the creator's laptop.

**Status:** Supported for that run only. **Edit:** “This 12-second clip exported in ten seconds on my laptop.” **Evidence:** the supplied recording. To keep the original wording would require evidence covering a much wider set of exports.
