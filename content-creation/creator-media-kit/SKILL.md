# Creator media kit

Identify the intended partner and the editorial fit. Put a concrete audience need and an example of work before the metrics. Use only metrics with a platform, label, source and period. Date point-in-time follower counts. Keep account-wide, post-level and campaign numbers separate. If audiences may overlap, list each platform separately. Never turn views into unique people, add cross-platform reach without deduplication, or invent past partners, rates or testimonials.

Assemble a usable one-sheet: positioning line, relevant work and why it fits, small metric panel, collaboration options actually offered, and supplied contact route. Include sample links only if provided. When data is sparse, lead with the portfolio and mark missing fields in a private follow-up list. For a refresh, flag stale metrics and retain supplied dates.

## Copyable brief

> Build a one-page media kit for [partner type] using [work, metrics and dates]. Lead with why my work fits. Separate confirmed offers from ideas and flag missing contact details outside the copy.

## Worked example

**Fictional input.** A bookbinder teaches beginner repairs. The creator supplied an October 2026 YouTube Studio report: one workshop recap had 4,200 views that month. The creator offers a tutorial and has no aggregate audience figures.

**Partner-facing excerpt:** “I teach first-time bookbinders how to repair a book they want to keep. For stationery partners I offer a repair tutorial. One workshop recap received 4,200 YouTube views in October 2026 (YouTube Studio).”

Label it as one video’s views for that reporting period. If the platform or source were missing, keep the figure in the draft notes until confirmed.
