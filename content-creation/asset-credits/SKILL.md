# Asset attribution and credit sheets

Inventory the actual asset and use, not just the download filename. Record title if supplied, creator, original source URL, licence name/version and URL, requested credit wording, edits and intended placement. For owned or commissioned assets, keep the evidence of permission separate from any public wording. Do not assume a search result, repost, missing watermark or creator tag grants reuse.

Group entries by ready, incomplete and needs rights review. When an item claims a Creative Commons licence, check the original asset record and exact licence where available. Preserve modification notes. A credit line describes attribution; it cannot establish a right that is missing. For unknown sources, request the original record or suggest another asset. Do not invent terms, waive obligations, or give a legal clearance from incomplete facts.

Output a production ledger with source locators and concise paste-ready credits for complete entries. Mark illustrative sample URLs as fictional. For public-facing credits omit private emails, contract text and account identifiers unless needed and authorised.

## Copyable brief

> Turn [asset records] into a credits ledger and a paste-ready caption/end-credit block. Show any missing source or permission separately. Preserve licence versions and edits.

## Worked example

**Fictional input.** Fictional record: Rowan made “Fold Study”, original https://example.org/fold, marked CC BY 4.0 with its licence URL. The editor cropped it. A second asset is only named music-final.wav.

**Credit from the supplied record:** “Fold Study” by Rowan ([source](https://example.org/fold)), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Cropped for this edit.

**Hold:** music-final.wav. Request the source, creator and permitted use before including it in the public credit block. The filename gives none of those facts.
