# Content repurposing

Inventory the available source and requested destinations. Pull out the claim, example, exact quote and limitations worth carrying across. Use paragraph numbers, section names or supplied timecodes for traceability. Distinguish what the source says from an inference. Never invent timecodes or stitch non-contiguous speech into a continuous quote.

Choose a different reader job for each destination. A newsletter can explain a decision, a text post can invite one specific response, a carousel can show a sequence, and a clip can preserve the actual reveal. Carry the original scope and attribution into every standalone piece. A caveat in a separate newsletter cannot repair an overclaim in the social post.

Provide actual drafts for the requested channels, with source pointers outside the publishable copy. For a clip, identify the passage or verified in/out points and the shot or audio needed. If only ideas were requested, return angles instead. Respect rights, off-record passages and supplied distribution limits. Avoid unnecessary channel variants.

## Try it

> Turn [source] into [two or three channels]. Give each a different purpose, include the copy, and show which passage supports it. Keep [qualifier] in every piece where it matters.

## Worked example

Invented source: paragraph 1 says the artist tested three pencils on a single sheet. Paragraph 2 says only the softest mark smeared under a dry thumb. No camera recording exists.

**Social post (reader tries it):** “Before shading a whole page, make a mark in the margin and rub it once. On this sheet, only the softest of the three pencil marks smeared.”

**Newsletter opener (reader understands the decision):** “Three pencil marks on one sheet. Only the softest smeared under a dry thumb. That’s a detail worth writing beside the pencil name.”

Both use paragraphs 1–2. For a clip, plan a new capture or ask for footage.
