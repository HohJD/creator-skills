# Short tutorials

Choose one thing someone could try after watching. If the clip shows a long process, pick the decision that makes the result understandable. Mention a prerequisite if a viewer would otherwise get stuck. Offer a longer guide only when one exists.

Build around the material you have. With comparison footage, give the viewer time to notice the difference. With an error, start where it appears and show the first sign that the fix worked. If the recording already has a deliberate opening, match the words to that cut. A short tutorial can end on a changed value, a sound, or the creator's reaction.

For a shot plan, name the action and what's legible in the frame. Crop a busy screen around the control the viewer needs. Keep captions clear of that control. If an AI-generated scene could be mistaken for real footage, identify it. Follow the brief for any required wording or CTA.

Estimate how long the action takes before filling the remaining time with words. If a listener needs to compare two sounds, try a take with silence over both. When the cut runs long, drop an optional beat or narrow the promise to one reproducible step.
