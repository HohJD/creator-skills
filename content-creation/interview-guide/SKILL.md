# Creator interview guide

Establish the editorial question, usable time, what the guest can speak to firsthand, and any agreed restrictions. Treat public claims as claims to verify. Do not write answers for a real guest or assume private experiences. For publication, identify recording and attribution arrangements still to settle.

Shape the guide around a few scenes or decisions the guest can recall. Start with a concrete, accessible question. Ask one thing at a time. Follow a vague claim with a request for a particular moment, object or action. Offer branching follow-ups that depend on what the guest says. Save space to clarify names, spellings, dates, quotations and what may be used.

Return a host-ready running order. Label indispensable questions and optional follow-ups. Keep research notes off the mic. For a very short slot, one well-developed story can be enough.

## Try it

> Prepare a [minutes]-minute interview with [guest] about [decision]. Give me a timed running order, one conditional follow-up per section and a closing fact check.

## Worked example

Fictional brief: eight minutes with a bicycle mechanic about a bell that sounded dull. Cause unknown.

**0–2 min:** “Take me back to the first time you rang it. What did you hear?” If the answer is abstract, ask for the sound in their own words.

**2–6 min:** “What did you check first?” If the first check ruled something out, ask what convinced them to move on. If the cause is still unknown, ask which observation would narrow it down.

**6–8 min:** “What should a listener pay attention to in the two recordings?” Confirm the part name and how the clips can be credited.
