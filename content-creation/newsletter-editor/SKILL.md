# Creator newsletters

Identify who subscribed, what they came for and the one thing this issue delivers. Lead with the useful detail or a scene from the supplied material. Keep a personal aside when it earns its place. Preserve required wording and the author's voice. Never manufacture urgency, a first-person anecdote, a discount, an attachment or a destination URL.

The subject should still make sense by itself. Use the preheader for an extra detail that makes opening worthwhile. Build the body around the actual takeaway. Put any action beside the reason to take it, and make the link label tell readers what opens. An issue can finish on a complete thought if the brief has no CTA. When a link is missing, flag it outside the send-ready copy or use clearly identified draft markup if requested.

Supply the requested fields in reading order, keeping editorial notes separate. If the brief is for an existing issue, retain its sections unless they get in the way. Review the subject and preview together on a narrow display and read the body once with images hidden.

## Try it

> Turn my [notes] into a short newsletter for [subscribers]. Give me a subject, a preheader that adds something new, and a body under [length]. The only link you may use is [URL or none].

## Worked example

Fictional note: a maker tests the same blue glaze on white and red clay; it appears darker on the red tile. No link or special offer.

**Subject:** One blue glaze, two test tiles

**Preheader:** The same blue looks darker on the red tile.

**Body:** Same glaze, two different clay bodies. The blue looks darker on the red tile. If you’re keeping a glaze notebook, write the clay body beside the colour. “Blue” alone won’t tell you which sample to reach for.

If the maker supplies photos, give each tile an informative label so readers can tell which is which with images disabled.
